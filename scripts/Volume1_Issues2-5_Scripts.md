## What This Story Teaches (own page, framed, after the last story page)

God made the first man with care, forming him from the ground and breathing life into him (Genesis 2:7). He gave him a good home, good work, and one clear command to obey (Genesis 2:15–17). God saw that it was “not good for the man to be alone,” so he made the woman and brought her to him (Genesis 2:18, 22). Jesus pointed back to this very chapter when he taught about marriage: “What therefore God has joined together, don’t let man tear apart” (Matthew 19:6). The story shows that people were made on purpose, for each other, and to live under God’s word. As the psalm says, “I am fearfully and wonderfully made” (Psalm 139:14).

## What This Story Teaches (own page, framed, after the last story page)

The serpent did not force the woman; he questioned what God had said and then denied it: “You won’t really die” (Genesis 3:4). The New Testament warns about the same danger, “as the serpent deceived Eve in his craftiness” (2 Corinthians 11:3). Sin started with wanting what God had forbidden, just as James describes: “each one is tempted when he is drawn away by his own lust and enticed” (James 1:14). Afterward the man and woman hid and blamed others. The Bible later explains that through one man “sin entered into the world” (Romans 5:12). Yet the chapter does not end in silence: God still spoke to them, and he made them garments of skins and clothed them (Genesis 3:21).

## What This Story Teaches (own page, framed, after the last story page)

Two brothers brought offerings, and God respected Abel’s. The Bible later explains why: “By faith Abel offered to God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain” (Hebrews 11:4). God warned Cain before he sinned: “sin crouches at the door. Its desire is for you, but you are to rule over it” (Genesis 4:7). Cain did not listen, and anger became murder. The apostle John uses this story to teach a simple command: “we should love one another” (1 John 3:11), “unlike Cain” (1 John 3:12). Even after this sorrow, God gave Adam and Eve another son, Seth, and people “began to call on Yahweh’s name” (Genesis 4:26).

## What This Story Teaches (own page, framed, after the last story page)

Genesis 5 reads like a list of names, but it carries a message. Again and again it says “then he died,” showing that what God told Adam came true. Yet the line goes on, and in the middle of it stands Enoch, who “walked with God” (Genesis 5:24). Hebrews explains that before he was taken “he had been well pleasing to God” (Hebrews 11:5), and it says, “Without faith it is impossible to be well pleasing to him” (Hebrews 11:6). The chapter ends with Noah, whose father hoped he would bring comfort (Genesis 5:29). Psalm 90:12 gives a fitting prayer: “teach us to count our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom.”
